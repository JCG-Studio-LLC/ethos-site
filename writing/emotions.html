From Diane

Emotions

Diane Clark, LSCSW, CCTP · March 2021

Emotions. Such tricky things. We feel overwhelmed by them, driven by them. Or we detach and avoid them completely. Sometimes they happen as a result of experiences. Often they just happen with no explanation. We can’t think them away. Good thoughts and positive thinking help but that alone is not the answer.

Often, we have a feeling, which is basically a chemical reaction in our brains and then we have thoughts about that feeling that cause us more feelings and more thoughts. We can get in feeling loops, anger, shame, rumination, anxiety. People talk about racing thoughts, all consuming thoughts, inability to control thoughts.

I have several analogies I use to help gain perspective on emotions. One is of the ocean. The ocean has moods. Some days are calm, some are active, and some are completely rough, even dangerous. We can’t control the ocean. We can notice its moods and respond to it in helpful ways. When playing in the ocean, it is imperative to be aware of the waves, to turn toward them, observe them with curiosity, prepare for them. But they can’t be stopped. We can dig our feet into the sand and resist, causing the wave to break over us, but we risk being drowned. We can ignore them, risking them breaking over us and beating us down. Or we can turn toward the waves, jump a little or even dive into them and let them carry us gently to shore.

Another helpful analogy is to think of a creek. After a rain, the creek can become very full, the water rushing over the rocks. We can easily get swept away, feeling ourselves beat against the rocks, carried downstream helplessly. We can try to dam up the water to no avail. Or we can get onto the bank and watch the water go by, with curiosity. Our thoughts can become like leaves we toss in and watch float away.

One of the benefits that clients seek when receiving therapy is to get help in naming their feelings. In addition, I help clients notice emotions in their bodies, for example, tense necks, hot faces, fullness in the chest. Clients can then treat their feelings with curiosity and learn to manage them through a variety of strategies. Clients gain perspective about their feelings, manage instead of ignore or try to stop them.
