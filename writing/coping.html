Coping

Diane Clark, LSCSW, CCTP · March 2021

Over the years of doing therapy, I have learned so many coping skills. There are strategies for overall self care and wellness. There are emergency calm strategies. There are specific activities to create specific chemical responses. These strategies are important and effective. Even as a person seeks assistance through medications and therapy for depression, anxiety, trauma, they can employ these strategies alongside. They are good for everyone, not just those suffering from mental health issues.

Dopamine is the reward chemical. You get it when you’ve done a good job or accomplished something. Making to do lists and crossing items off gives you dopamine. Some other ways to increase dopamine are creativity, drinking coffee, green tea, human contact, eating, getting a massage, music, self care activities, sunlight, and sleep.

Serotonin is a calming chemical. Eating complex carbs, cycling, essential oils, happy memories, meditation, running, probiotics, therapy, and visualizing are ways to increase serotonin.

Endorphins are feel good chemicals we get from exercise. Changing temperatures, dark chocolate, giving gifts, laughter, kindness, and sitting in a sauna also can increase endorphins.

Oxytocin is the love chemical. Affection, acupuncture, compliments, discovering new things, Lovingkindness meditations, volunteering, working together, time with friends and social media can increase oxytocin levels.

There are also coping strategies that target specific trauma reactions. Accepting our emotions helps with all of the trauma reactions of fight, flight, freeze and fawn. Breathing, grounding, rocking in a rocking chair, safe connections and connecting with the self help with the fight response. Pets, safe places, touch, grounding, self compassion and weighted blankets help with flight and freeze. Boundaries, safe connections, self awareness, and more help with fawning.

There are so many other strategies for overall wellness that help us cope. Increasing water intake, getting enough sleep, eating well, vitamins, gratitude, prayer, changing our mindset, comedy, conversation, decluttering, going for walks, hot baths, pets, mindfulness, resting, sex, visualizing, yoga, journalling, worship, and so much more, help us manage the many emotions that life creates.

If you aren’t sure what works for you, try them! I recommend checking in with yourself regularly and doing something that helps physically, mentally, emotionally, relationally and spiritually every day.
