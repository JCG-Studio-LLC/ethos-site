How to Break Up with Your Therapist

Diane Clark, LSCSW, CCTP · January 2022

The therapeutic relationship is a unique one. One sided, it is all about the client, their feelings, their story, their needs. Though therapists use some self disclosure, the focus is on the client. But that doesn’t mean that therapists don’t have feelings. Many clients, unable or unwilling to express their desire to end therapy, just stop showing up. Though professionals, therapists are people too.

Ideally, the end of therapy is discussed starting in the first session. Sometimes clients seek out therapy for specific, well defined reasons, to work through a life circumstance or relationship, or to find relief from specific symptoms. The therapist and the client together write goals. When those goals are achieved, therapy is done.

But it isn’t always as clear as that. Often, there are underlying issues, trauma or unresolved childhood or other issues that are uncovered in therapy. New treatment goals are then created or current ones rewritten. Ideally, these are conversations that the client and therapist have together.

But what if you are just done with therapy? Perhaps it costs too much or is too painful during a particular time in your life. Maybe therapy wasn’t what you expected or you aren’t pleased with the direction that therapy is going, or you don’t feel a fit with the therapist… Whatever the reason you don’t want to continue in therapy, I promise that the therapist wants to have that conversation.

Therapists are used to clients ending therapy. Ideally, this is anticipated, discussed, even celebrated together. Sort of like a graduation. Therapists and clients discuss the progress, the growth, some of the lessons learned. It is a transition. It is imperative that this conversation happens, for both the client and for the therapist.

So what do you say to your therapist if you think you want to stop therapy? Use the direct approach. “I am thinking that I want to stop therapy and I wanted to talk to you about it.”

Please, don’t just no show. Don’t just stop coming, leaving your therapist hanging. Even if you aren’t happy with your therapy, your therapist would much rather hear that than nothing at all.
